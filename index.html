Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about APS scores, how they're calculated, which universities use which scoring system, and how to improve your results.

What is an APS score?
The Admission Point Score (APS) is a numerical value calculated from your Matric (NSC) results. South African universities use it as a primary screening tool for admission. Each subject mark is converted to points on a scale, and the total determines which programmes you qualify for. Different universities use different scales and rules.
Is Life Orientation included?
It depends on the university. Standard APS (UP, UJ): Life Orientation is excluded — only your best 6 other subjects count. Wits: Life Orientation is included but on a reduced scale (max 4 points instead of 8). UCT & Stellenbosch: Life Orientation is excluded.
Why is my Wits score different from my Standard APS?
Wits uses an 8-point scale (vs. the standard 7-point scale) and awards bonus points: +2 for English and +2 for Mathematics if you score 60% or above. Mathematical Literacy does not receive the Maths bonus. Wits also includes Life Orientation on a special reduced scale. This means Wits scores are typically higher than Standard APS.
Does meeting the minimum APS guarantee admission?
No. Meeting the minimum APS makes you eligible to apply, but admission also depends on subject-specific requirements, NBT scores (for some universities), available places, and faculty selection criteria. Always check the specific programme requirements on the university's official website.
Can I use my Grade 11 marks?
Yes! Use the Grade 11 toggle at the top of the calculator. Many universities accept early applications based on Grade 11 results. Your final APS will be recalculated using your Grade 12 (NSC) results. Grade 11 marks give you an estimate to start planning.
How does the UCT Faculty Points Score work?
UCT uses a Faculty Points Score (FPS) instead of the standard 7-point APS scale. Your FPS is the sum of your raw percentage marks for your best 6 subjects (excluding Life Orientation). The maximum is 600 (6 subjects x 100%). For example, if your top 6 subjects average 75%, your FPS would be 450. This system rewards consistently high marks across all subjects rather than just meeting level thresholds.
What's the difference between Mathematics and Mathematical Literacy?
Mathematics (Pure Maths) is required for most Science, Engineering, Commerce, and Health Sciences degrees. It covers algebra, calculus, and abstract problem-solving. Mathematical Literacy focuses on practical, everyday maths skills. Many competitive programmes (Medicine, Engineering, Actuarial Science, BSc, BCom) do not accept Mathematical Literacy. At Wits, Maths (not Math Lit) qualifies for the +2 bonus if you score 60% or above. Always check your target programme's specific requirements.
How many subjects do I need?
Most students take 7 subjects in Matric: 4 compulsory (Home Language, First Additional Language, Mathematics or Math Literacy, and Life Orientation) plus 3 electives. Some students take 8 or 9 subjects. For APS calculation, universities use your best 6 subjects excluding Life Orientation (except Wits, which includes LO on a reduced scale). If you take more than 7, only your best marks count.
Can I compare multiple careers?
Yes! The Gap Analysis tab lets you compare up to 3 careers side by side. Select a university and programme for each career slot, enter your marks once, and see exactly what you need for each one. Use the "Add another career" button to add Career 2 and Career 3. This makes it easy to see which programmes are within reach and where you need to focus your effort.
